**Divergences**

The differences in these articles reflect what computational methods are best suited for their specific objectives.

The articles chosen primarily use computation for analysis, but also involve some augmentation. #### **Step 3: Trends, Divergences, and Silences**

**Trends**

The articles emphasized scalability and quantification from the use of computational tools in terms of treating language as measurable data to reveal broader cultural patterns. They primarily prioritize identifying certain patterns over deeper interpretation of the findings themselves. 

**Divergences**

The main divergence is the role computation plays. Some approaches use computation to analyze data and make arguments about cultural patterns, while others use it as a tool for creating and communicating content, allowing readers to produce their own interpretations. There are also differences in data structure, where some rely on fixed datasets while others depend on user-generated data that is constantly changing. There is also a difference in who controls the interpretation. In some cases, the models generate the main insights, while in others, the computational tools support the individuals to create meanings themselves.

**Silences**

The articles leave out how exactly the different categories are being defined. For example, what counts as “game-related” or “moral-emotional,” and how those definitions shape the results. There is also a lack of discussion about what the data leaves out, such as tone, context, or the perspectives of individuals not included in the dataset that may be important for the specific article objectives. Another common silence is the loss of cultural nuance. By transforming language and behavior into measurable features, there are complexities in human nature that are somewhat lost like sarcasm, intent, or cultural meaning. Questions also may arise in terms of whether computation is exactly the best or even the only method to go through computation, it seems that the findings are only being reduced to numbers, excluding the full cultural aspect behind them. 

**Group Reflection**

As a group, we agree that computation is useful for analyzing large datasets that would not be as plausible to do individually at the same rate and scalable measure, but we also see the trade-offs that come with computation. Our articles show tension, scale and nuance, to which gaining broad patterns can mean losing deeper cultural contexts. So, even if the patterns are recognized through computation, it may limit how deeply those patterns are understood.
